## Reused from M0: the loop, with one addition
Every ticket runs M0's loop ("Finishing M0", item 3; ADR 0041; `orchestrate-wave`):
1. `acceptance-writer` commits failing tests (`acceptance:` commits, 20 minutes) before the builder starts. It runs them against a throwaway implementation, and the orchestrator rules its "not pinned" list first.
2. The builder works to its budget.
3. **Every ticket that changes `web/**` walks its screens by keyboard itself before its PR,** against `docs/design/m1-screens.md`'s checklist (CLAUDE.md's lesson of 28 Sep). Then one `pr-reviewer` review, with `ux-critic` beside it: the walk for UI tickets, the words-only gate for backend tickets that word codes.
4. At most two fix rounds. A later finding is filed, unless it is a security hole scoring 75 or more, a crash or false statement a QS meets, or a regression the first fix round introduced (`fix-regression`, the owner, 5 Oct 2026).
5. Every finding of 50 or more, or a repeated class, leaves a committed check.
6. The orchestrator merges `main` in, posts `design-gate` from an independent verdict, runs and accepts `real-drawings` under the accept rule, runs `python -m scripts.merge_ready <PR>`, and merges.

Also kept:
- **Adversary rounds after each merge wave:** Sonnet 5.5 agents, one surface each. Any finding of 50 or more is re-run by an Opus `refuter` before anyone acts on it. M1's surfaces, in order:
  - the scorer and the key fence (element-level leaks);
  - the confirm service and the `attrs` walls;
  - the money chain (rounding, currency, allowance replacement, the piling contract);
  - the recognisers on hostile DWG;
  - the Held-out instance's separation;
  - the 3D viewer's inputs.
- **Time:** elapsed against budget in every message to a builder. Suite output is kept under `.private/work/` (pytest `-rf`).
- **Effort** is medium unless the ticket reads drawings, meets hostile input or builds a security wall (ADR 0041 §8; CLAUDE.md). Each high ticket names its reason in a word in the tables below.

**Session 16's lessons (A21; binding from wave 0):**

- **How:**
  1. After the builder's first READY, the orchestrator runs the posting run with `--score` on its head.
  2. It fans out agents, one local session each (counted against the six local sessions), **each on a different failing (family, storey) or sheet**, running `--no-post` one at a time under the lock.
  3. Their fixes land on the ticket's branch one at a time, and each is re-scored.
- **What is looped:** both reads (C1) are scored once the with-profile read is built; before that, the unknown-office read alone. The ticket's own family's n / N is what the loop drives. Every other family's n / N is a guard: no family may lose a passing (family, storey) without a reason the reviewer judges.
- **Stop (ADR 0030:10):** the ticket's n / N stops improving **on both reads** (on the one read, before the profile exists): two scored heads in a row gain nothing on either.
- **Budget cut (CLAUDE.md, not ADR 0030):** the loop has at most 90 minutes inside the ticket's budget (M0.md, "Finishing"). At the cut, each (family, storey) still failing is filed as an issue on the milestone, counts only, and the ticket merges on the reviewer's verdict.
- **Record:** every scored head goes in the M1 milestone issue, counts only.

## M1's Answer Keys at element level (the default; fixed before any result)
The spec settles who holds the keys and what the scorer returns (M1.md:1102-1132; ADRs 0005, 0026, 0030). It does not settle the element key's schema, the join, what n / N means per element, or the contradiction key. The default below settles all four. It is written into M1-01's contract and never tuned after a result.

**Three kinds of key, by set:**
| Set | Element rows | Counts per family per storey | Hand Takeoff zone lines | Contradictions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sample Project (`sample-project`) | yes, from its generating inputs | derived from the rows | none (ADR 0005 History) | yes, the planted list |
| Edison (`edison`) | none | yes (M1-05) | yes (the team engineer's Hand Takeoff) | none |
| Held-out Set | none | yes, counted by the key user's side | yes | none |

Edison and the Held-out Sets keep counts plus zone lines, because their truth is a count and a takeoff, not a model. A size error shows in the zone quantities (±1 % on concrete). **What counts cannot see:** a wrong element in the right count (a substitution). So on Edison and the Held-out Set a (family, storey) that passes on counts is reported as passing only if its zone's lines for that family also pass (gate mode, C3).

**One file per set.** The element key is its own file, `<keys>/<set>.elements.json`, beside the sheet key M0 put in custody, which is never edited. The set names are custody's (`SETS=(sample-project edison)`, `keys-custody.sh:39`). The drafts sit in `.private/work/keys-draft/m1/<set>.elements.json`.

- Values are decimal strings. Positions are in the **grid frame**: the origin is the key's named grid intersection, the axes run along the two grid directions, and the offsets are measured along them. A grid line's `angle_deg` is its direction in the drawing's plan, so a rotated grid is written as drawn.
- The storey key `""` means "no storey" (piles and pile caps), in rows and counts alike.
- `counts_as` maps each planted kind to the Question codes that hit it. It is drafted by M1-02 from C10's table as fixed on this page, before any result, and held by the key user. The scorer reads it from the key, never from engine code (the scorer imports nothing of the project, `tools/scorer/score.py:14-16`).
- A beam's `role` is `main` (supported by columns or walls) or `secondary` (supported by main beams). The span rule for counting beams one per span is: a main beam's span runs between its supporting columns or walls; a secondary beam's between the main beams it sits on. M1-05's analyst brief and M1-39 use the same rule.

**The element join** (ADR 0015's identity, expressed in numbers):
1. The scorer converts the export's positions into the key's grid frame, using the export's **registered** grid (C1's `grid`, one frame per Building) and the key's origin labels. If the export has no line with an origin label, every element of the set fails with the reason "grid frame not found".
2. Candidates must match on family and storey key.
3. Joins are one to one, nearest first, within these radii:
   - pile and pile cap: 300 mm (the cap by its polygon's centroid);
   - column: 150 mm between centres;
   - shear wall and lift core: axis overlap ≥ 50 % of the key's length, with axis offset ≤ 150 mm;
   - beam: both segment ends ≤ 300 mm;
   - slab: polygon IoU ≥ 0.8 (for the join only; the slab's area is scored below);
   - slab edge: overlap of the host edge ≥ 50 %, offset ≤ 300 mm;
   - storey: slab level ≤ 25 mm;
   - grid line: equal label after normalising case and whitespace, else parallel within 1° and ≤ 50 mm.
4. A mark is never used to join.

**The scored attributes, per family** (the qs-critic's list; a joined element passes only if every one is within tolerance):
| Family | Scored attributes |
|---|---|
| pile | diameter, cut-off level, toe level |
| pile cap | plan area (from `vx.pile_cap.polygon`), depth, top level |
| column | section b and d (or diameter), per storey |
| shear wall | thickness, length, opening area |
| lift core | each wall's thickness and length |
| beam | width, overall depth, level offset |
| slab | area, void area, thickness, level |
| slab edge | kind, projection, thickness |
| storey | slab level, finished floor level |
| grid line | offset |

**Attribute tolerances:**
- section sizes, pile diameter, wall and slab thickness: ≤ 1 mm (the exact inch conversion, ADR 0008);
- levels: ≤ 5 mm;
- lengths: ≤ max(1 %, 25 mm);
- areas (cap plan area, slab area, void area, opening area): ≤ 1 %.

**What n / N means:**
- *Element rows:* N = the key's element count per family per storey. n = elements joined with every scored attribute within tolerance. Also reported: joined by position only, and phantoms (export elements that join nothing), counted apart. There are no exclusions in an unattended read. Excluded elements keep their place in N in the product (M1.md:308), and the project scoring export (C13) counts them apart.
- *Counts only:* n = Σ min(export count, key count) and phantoms = Σ max(0, export − key), per (family, storey).
- "Changed attributes per family" (ADR 0030) counts the scored attributes above that differ, run to run.

**Planted contradictions:** a hit is an export Question whose family and storey match and whose code is in the key's `counts_as` list for the kind. The scorer reports "raised k of K".

**The zone lines** (Edison, Held-out): `{"zones": [{"zone": "typical", "storeys": ["floor_3"], "lines": [{"item_code": "<a BOQ Item of the signed Rule Set>", "billing_unit": "cft", "quantity": "1234.56"}]}]}`, in the same `<set>.elements.json`. The scorer maps export storeys to zones from the key (M1.md:1125). Zone lines are scored **only in gate mode**, against the product's scoring export (C13): a read's export (C1) carries no BOQ Items and no rates. Each line is compared with the finish-line table's tolerance (M1.md:1437-1465). A zone's ৳ is the key's quantities priced at the scoring export's rates, ±2 %.

- **The Sample Project's rows** (M1-02): reduced from the generating inputs by a script run as the key user (handed over outside the repo). Fallback, if the inputs lack them: the same analyst route as Edison.
- **Held-out counts and every Hand Takeoff:** the team engineer on the key user's side. Never a build agent.
- No builder is pointed at `.private/work/keys-draft/m1/`.

## The contracts fixed here (so tickets in one wave can build against each other)

### Units: one rule
- **Inside the product** (`ElementState.attrs`, `RebarBar`, Measurement Lines, `measurement` and `boq`): SI, as exact decimal strings, never a float (ADR 0008): metres, m², m³, MPa, kg. Each Attribute Definition names its one `storage_unit` (C8).
- **At the scoring interface** (the export C1 and the keys C2): lengths and levels in millimetres; areas are computed by the scorer from polygons in mm.
- **Conversion happens in exactly three places:** `engine.export.build` (a candidate's drawing units to mm, for C1); `takeoff`'s confirm service (drawing units or the QS's typed unit to the storage unit; a position becomes metres in the Building frame from the file's units and the view's confirmed scale, M1-12's `confirm_scale`; A7); and `boq` (SI to Billing Units and Display Units, by `BoqItemBillingUnit`). The scorer, the measurer and `rates` never convert. **Measurement Lines carry SI only;** `measurement` holds no fixed billed system (A16).

### C3 The scorer's answers (`vx-score`, stdlib, system Python 3.12)

### C5 The chain run (M1-10a; A2, A3, A4, A6, A20)
`engine.families.chain.run(reading: SetReading, views: Sequence[ViewArtefact], confirmed: ConfirmedFacts, profile: ProfileParts | None, *, jev: JevPort | None, steps: Sequence[str] | None = None) -> ChainResult` (C4) is **the only composition of families,** for the harness, the real-drawing check and the product alike (#551). The slice composed them in `vextrus/takeoff/services/frame_read.py` instead, so the harness and the scorer could not measure what the product read.
- **Order and feeding:** the families run in Takeoff Step order (`steps` limits which). Each is given the earlier families' facts: confirmed ones where the QS has confirmed them, else their **best candidates** (Trace kind `best_candidate`), never a key. The chain registers the grid once and hands the `RegisteredGrid` to later families.
- **Which views a family reads** (A3): only views whose kind is in `manifest.view_kinds` and whose Step 1 CoverageSteps include one of `manifest.reads_steps`. A section or a schedule never reaches a plan reader (a test). This replaces the slice's `_READS_PLANS_OF` in `takeoff`; in the slice every family read every confirmed view and raised one Question per view.
- **Placing a view on its sheet** (A6): one function, `engine.recognise.place.place(view, sheet) -> PlacedView | Unplaced`, built from the stored anchors on #535's paper model. A view that cannot be placed raises `engine.view.not_placed (view)` once, not once per family. No family holds placement code (the slice had two copies).
- **One Element read on several views** (A4): candidates of one family that share an identity (the family's identity rule, within its tolerance) across views become one candidate carrying every view's anchors. Values that disagree become one `conflict` QuestionRaised (21c's two-plans code). Which view governs a storey's piece comes from the ViewPlacement's meaning (C19). `live_model.apply` still refuses a repeated identity; the chain, not `act`, resolves identity.
- **Storeys** are ranked once, by `engine.recognise.storeys`; no other module copies the ranking.
- **Jev:** with `jev=None` (the sandbox has no network), a judgement request takes code's first-ranked option and is counted on the candidate (`judgements`).
- **Checks:** the chain attaches its candidates to the reading (`SetReading.candidates`, a new field, empty before the chain runs; M1-10a changes `engine/recognise/types.py`), and `engine.check.catalogue.run_all` then runs every set Check over it.
- **The product's read** (A20; M1-11c): the product runs the chain over every step at once with best candidates, so every step has Proposals early. `takeoff`'s recognise service only maps a `ChainResult` to Proposals and Questions under C9's re-read rule; it composes nothing. A Confirmation that changes a fact a later family read re-runs those families under the same rule (a test: editing a grid line's offset moves the column Proposals).
- **A lint** (M1-10a) refuses any import of `engine.families.*.recognise` under `vextrus/`.

### C14 The Material Schedule row (M1-16)
```json
{"resource": "cement_opc", "group": {"code": "rates.group.cement"}, "unit": "bag",
 "stage": "slab_casting", "storey": "floor_2", "quantity_net": "398.20", "wastage_pct": "3",
 "quantity_gross": "411", "amount": {"amount": "…", "currency": "XXX"},
 "diameter_mm": null, "grade": null, "assumed_split": false, "allowance": false, "lead_days": 7,
 "first_needed_stage": "piling", "items": ["RCC-SLB-1:1.5:3", "RCC-BM-1:1.5:3"]}
```
- `quantity_gross` is `quantity_net` × (1 + wastage) rounded **up** in the resource's unit, per cell (398.20 × 1.03 = 410.146, up to 411). Totals are the sum of the rounded cells.
- Rebar rows carry `grade` and `diameter_mm`, with ton totals per grade and diameter, and `assumed_split: true` where the Rebar is by ratio.
- Allowance rows sit in `storey: null` ("not yet by floor"), `allowance: true`.
- Material-and-Labour materials never appear, piling's included when that contract is chosen.

### C19 Storeys, levels and View Placements (M1-32a; M1-08's placement service; M1-32b's level reader; A5, O3, O4)
Step 3 is the root of every later step's storeys and heights. The slice had no data path for it: storeys came from M0's view storey keys, levels were typed, `storey_id` and storey heights were not applied until late, M0's `excludes_storey` answer was not applied, and plan views went into Proposals while `ViewPlacement` and `ViewPlacementStorey` stood unused.

- **Sources, in order:**
  1. Step 1's confirmed view storeys give the storey list and its order, ranked once by `engine.recognise.storeys`. Tanks are never storeys.
  2. Level marks, sections and elevations give slab and finished levels, wherever level marks exist (M1-32b's reader).

- **`StateChange.storey_id`** is required for every family bound to a storey (C7).

  - `GET storeys` → `{storeys: [{id, key, name, order, slab_level_m, finished_level_m, height_m, basis}], view_placements: [{view_id, sheet: {id, number, title}, meaning, storeys: [{id, name}]}]}`
