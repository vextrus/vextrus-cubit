## Problem Statement

A Dhaka Developer's QS gets a consultant's 2D structural drawings and must turn them into the frame's
quantities and money: piles, pile caps, columns, shear walls, the lift core, beams and slabs, with
their concrete, formwork and rebar. Today that is weeks of measurement sheets in Excel, read sheet by
sheet, where one element is measured on three sheets and a wrong schedule cell passes silently. The
MD waits for the whole takeoff before seeing any figure, and when the figure comes nobody can show
where a number was read. Rebar is either guessed from a thumb rule or counted bar by bar by hand.

After M0 the QS can upload the Drawing Set and see every sheet split, named and legible, but nothing
is measured and nothing is priced. Vextrus Cubit failed exactly here: it measured almost nothing,
refused any line missing one fact, and had no money at all (docs/postmortem.md, causes 2 and 4).

And every office draws its own way. A reader fitted to one consultant office reads nothing of the
next office's set (docs/research/edison-check-session-02.md), while a Dhaka QS receives sets from many
offices, most of them never seen before.

## Solution

The QS runs the Takeoff Steps that build the frame, in building order: sheets; general notes and
specification; storeys and levels, from the structural and the architectural sections and
elevations; grid; piles and pile caps; columns, shear walls and the lift core; beams; slabs with their
slab-edge members. Each step reads the consultant office's way of drawing from its Drafting Profile:
proposed on that office's first set, with its evidence, confirmed by the QS, and reused on the office's
next. In each step the machine proposes every element it read, with its Trace; the QS confirms the
agreeing ones in bulk (a Storey Band at once, though each storey's column is its own Element) and the
exceptions one by one; a fact the machine could not read, or a Check that fired, becomes one Question
at the point of need, answered once for every element it unblocks. A step may close with Questions
still open, its held elements priced at their best candidate and marked "awaiting answer". The frame
grows in the Live Model's 3D after each Confirmation, coloured by status, with the tools a QS checks
it by: measure and area, an element's dimensions and clear spans, sections with dimensions, and
properties and filters by any Attribute. Each Element carries its As designed Attributes, mapped to
IFC and classified by Uniclass.

From the first Confirmation the Priced BOQ shows a whole-building figure: every Takeoff Step not yet
closed, and every MEP Part not yet read, carries a marked allowance, held as consumption per sft of
Gross Floor Area and priced at the Developer's Market Prices; each closed step replaces its allowance
with its measured figure. The frame's concrete, formwork and rebar are measured by the pinned Rule Set
version (a pure function of the As designed facts), priced by Rate Analyses on the Developer's
editable Market Prices (starting from PWD's SoR 2022 Dhaka prices and Low starter labour rates,
labelled so), with Labour Contracts as their own lines. Column rebar is read from the drawing; the
rest is by Rebar Ratio with an "assumed" diameter split, and every rebar figure shows its Rebar Basis.
The Material Schedule shows what to buy by Construction Stage and floor, in quantities and ৳. Every
figure opens its Trace.

M1 is done when the owner walks it on the Development Sets and on one Held-out Set, the Held-out Set
read first as an unknown office's set and then with a Drafting Profile; the figures agree with the
Hand Takeoff within tolerance, scored blind; a Drafting Profile learnt on Edison has been confirmed by
the QS; and a QS who did not build Vextrus has run a timed Takeoff.

## User Stories

### Starting the Takeoff (QS)
1. As a QS, I want the Takeoff to open on a step rail showing the fourteen Takeoff Steps in building
   order and, after them, the MEP Parts, with M1's steps open and the rest shown with their allowance,
   so that I always know where I am and what is still an allowance.
2. As a QS, I want to type the Building's Gross Floor Area from the architect's area statement when I
   start, so that the whole-building figure appears from my first Confirmation.
3. As a QS, I want to see which Rule Set version my project is measured by, by name and number, so
   that I know the rules behind every figure.
4. As a QS, I want to read every Measurement Rule my project uses in words, with its source (IS 1200,
   PWD or practice), so that I can settle an argument about a figure by pointing at its rule.
5. As a QS, I want each Takeoff Step to open only when the steps it depends on are closed (storeys
   and grid before any member; columns before beams; beams before slabs), so that no element is
   placed on facts I have not accepted.
6. As a QS, I want the drafting conventions of the office that drew the set (which layers hold which
   members, how marks, sizes and levels are written, where the notes and schedules are) proposed with
   their evidence, confirmed once, and reused on that office's next set, so that I am not asked the
   same thing on every project.
7. As a QS, I want to see which office's Drafting Profile a set is read with, and to correct the office
   if it is wrong, so that a set is never read with another office's conventions.
8. As a QS, I want a convention that would raise the same Question on most sheets asked once, as a
   convention, so that one office habit never costs me fifty answers.
9. As a QS, I want to draw a sheet or a view the machine missed, or split a wrong one, by hand, recorded
   as typed by me, so that a set Vextrus has never seen still gets every sheet read.

### General notes and specification (step 2)
10. As a QS, I want each general-notes fact (concrete strength or Mix per element class, rebar grade,
    clear cover per element class, lap length, pile data the notes state) proposed with the sentence it
    came from, so that I confirm the specification in one pass.
11. As a QS, I want a fact the notes do not state proposed as "not stated", naming the Rule Set default
    that will apply, so that nothing is assumed silently.
12. As a QS, I want to choose once which BOQ Item a stated concrete strength is built as (a site Mix or
    ready-mix), pre-picked from my Developer's previous choice, so that each member lands on the item
    my Developer actually buys.
13. As a QS, I want a lap length stated in the notes to make column laps "from the drawing", and an
    unstated one to fall back to 40 × bar diameter under rule R3, so that the Rebar Basis says which.
14. As a QS, I want notes kept in their own file, in columns of numbered sections, with tables written
    as free text (strengths in psi, laps in bar diameters by member, position and bar size), read into
    the same facts, so that an office's way of writing notes does not lose them.

### Storeys and levels (step 3)
15. As a QS, I want storeys and their levels proposed from the level lines and marks in the structural
    and architectural sections and elevations, each storey with its slab level and its finished floor
    level, sorted by elevation and named once, so that every screen uses one name per floor and a set
    whose structural drawings carry few levels is still levelled.
16. As a QS, I want a level mark that disagrees with the level lines asked as a Question (a local
    level such as a sunk slab, or a drafting mistake), so that real sunk and raised zones are never
    erased as typos.
17. As a QS, I want each plan view placed on the explicit list of storeys it shows ("typical floor
    plan, 2nd–8th"), with a storey shared by two ranges asked, so that a typical plan counts for exactly
    its storeys. *(Review Q1: M0 confirms the storey words as the title states them; step 3 resolves
    the symbolic ones against the confirmed storeys: "typical" and "top" (never a fixed floor), and
    "below ground floor" as foundation to ground, and a Question where a basement is confirmed.
    Tanks and the underground reservoir are structures for step 10, never storeys, so no view is
    placed on a tank.)*
18. As a QS, I want level lines checked against their level labels, shown n / N, so that a wrong
    storey height is caught before any column is measured on it.
19. As a QS, I want two plans of one kind that claim the same storey (two beam layouts titled for one
    floor, a duplicated title), or a plan placed on a storey step 3 did not confirm, raised as a
    Question, so that no floor is measured twice or on a storey that does not exist. *(Review Q3: by
    M0's rule, two plans claim one storey only when their Discipline, subject and layer (top or
    bottom) all match; continuation sheets with one title are one group, never a Question.)*
20. As a QS, I want a storey whose finished level and slab level differ by other than the floor finish
    the notes state raised as a Question, so that the architect's and the engineer's levels are
    reconciled once.

### Grid (step 4)
21. As a QS, I want the grid read from each plan's own geometry and bubbles, and registered across
    views through shared intersections, so that every member on every sheet lands in one frame.
22. As a QS, I want a grid whose dimension text disagrees with its drawn spacing raised as a
    Question, so that a mis-scaled view is caught at the grid, not in the money.
23. As a QS, I want a grid line whose bubbles are present but whose line is missing on one plan
    proposed with that evidence, so that I decide rather than lose it.
24. As a QS, I want each view's scale proposed from its own dimensions (their text against their drawn
    length, with the insert scale and the dimension factor honoured) and confirmed with the grid, and a
    view whose dimensions fit no one scale asked, so that no member is measured at the wrong scale.

### Piles and pile caps (step 5, M1's part)
25. As a QS, I want piles proposed as a whole family (count, diameter, cut-off level, length from
    cut-off to toe) from the pile layout, pile schedule and notes, whether a pile is drawn as a circle
    or as a block and whether its mark names the pile or its type, so that piling is measured.
26. As a QS, I want the pile count shown n / N against the pile layout, so that a missed pile is
    visible.
27. As a QS, I want each pile cap proposed with its mark, plan size, depth and the piles inside it,
    and a cap whose piles do not match its type's pile count raised as a Question, so that a wrong cap
    type is caught.
28. As a QS, I want pile concrete to show the cut-off-to-toe length and the over-cast above cut-off
    as separate visible lines, and pile-head breaking as its own item, so that I see what is bought
    and what is broken off.

### Columns, shear walls and the lift core (step 6)
29. As a QS, I want each column proposed as one Element per storey at its grid intersection, grouped
    by Storey Band and mark, so that I confirm a band of a typical floor in one keystroke while each
    floor's piece keeps its own identity.
30. As a QS, I want a "confirm all agreeing" shortcut for the whole step, so that 86 agreeing columns
    take one act and I spend my time on the 3 that need me.
31. As a QS, I want the Confirmation bar to say what and why ("14 C2 columns selected on this plan.
    All 14 agree with the Column schedule and sit on grid points"), so that I know what I am
    confirming.
32. As a QS, I want a column whose drawn size, label and schedule disagree pulled out as an
    exception, with the likely answer pre-picked only when two or more sources agree and the card
    naming them, so that the machine never guesses for me.
33. As a QS, I want a blank schedule cell asked as one Question for every column it blocks, with the
    card saying first what answering does ("Answering confirms 4 columns = 300 cft"), so that I am
    never asked twice.
34. As a QS, I want an off-grid column placed by its offset from the nearest grid intersection, so
    that it keeps its identity like any other.
35. As a QS, I want shear walls and the lift core proposed per storey, grouped by Storey Band, with
    their thickness and length, the lift core known by its own walls and never by the nearest column
    mark, so that the core is measured, not forgotten.
36. As a QS, I want a label bound to a member of another kind (a column mark on the lift core) raised
    as a Question, so that a wrong binding never reaches the money.
37. As a QS, I want outlines of one family found on two layers of one sheet at different sizes asked
    (which layer is current), so that a stale layer never raises false disagreements or doubles a
    member.
38. As a QS, I want an element I exclude (X, with a reason) to stay in N, so that the count shows the
    gap and its reason.
39. As a QS, I want to edit a size (E) where I know better, recorded in the Trace as typed by me, so
    that my correction is visible and never re-derived.

### Column rebar from the drawing
40. As a QS, I want each column band's bars (main bars by count and diameter, ties by diameter and
    spacing) read from the column schedule, whether a table of text or a grid of drawn sections, so
    that column rebar comes from the drawing.
41. As a QS, I want laps measured as a separate visible line beside the net bars, so that I see what
    the laps cost.
42. As a QS, I want every length the drawing does not state (laps, hooks, anchorage) supplied by a

    read and what was assumed.
43. As a QS, I want bars that cannot fit inside the column section with its stated cover raised as a
    Question, so that a misread bar callout never reaches the money.
44. As a QS, I want a rebar-only Question to wait in the Questions tab without interrupting my
    Confirmation of the columns, so that sizes and money move on while one bar callout waits.
45. As a QS, I want a disagreement between a column schedule and a column section detail settled by
    a precedence I confirm once per consultant office, in its Drafting Profile, so that I am not asked
    on every column.

### Beams (step 7)
46. As a QS, I want beams proposed per span from the beam layout of each slab level, whether drawn as

47. As a QS, I want each beam's size from its own label or the beam schedule, span by span (one mark
    may carry different sizes on different spans), with an unlabelled beam asked, so that no size is
    invented.
48. As a QS, I want a beam whose span in a section differs from its plan span raised as a Question,

49. As a QS, I want a typical beam layout applied to the storeys it covers, so that I confirm it once.

### Slabs and slab-edge members (step 8)
50. As a QS, I want slab panels proposed per storey with their thickness, voids (lift, stair and

51. As a QS, I want an unstated slab thickness asked once per panel type, never blocking the beams, so

    Cubit).
52. As a QS, I want sunshades, drop walls, fins and cornices proposed with their slab, a projection
    of 100 mm or less average thickness classed as a sunshade, so that the ornamental class (d) is
    priced as PWD prices it.

### Drafting Profiles (ADR 0039)
- **What one holds:** one consultant office's conventions for one Discipline, as data: the sheet frame
  and the title block's field positions within it; sheet-number forms (bare in the title block,
  prefixed in references); the storey words its titles use; which layers hold which members, per sheet
  where they vary, drafter suffixes included; label patterns per family ("C1", "mark (b × d)", dotted
  marks) and whether a mark fixes a size; level-mark wording (signs in parentheses, the datum, marks
  relative to the roof); where the notes live (a file of their own, sections in columns) and how their
  tables are written (units such as psi; laps in bar diameters by member, position and bar-size band);
  how schedules are drawn (tables of text, per-sheet tables, grids of drawn sections); the plan-versus-
  section precedence for rebar (ADR 0010); the tolerances the office's drafting needs (how far a label
  may sit from its member). Conventions only, never drawing content: no sizes, counts, levels, titles
  or names of people or projects.
- **Learnt:** code proposes each convention from candidates it finds by geometry (an outline whose
  width equals its label's size; circles inside cap outlines; a text at the same place in every
  frame), Jev picking among candidates only where code cannot (ADR 0011). Each convention shows its
  evidence, the entities it rests on, by Trace.
- **Confirmed:** the QS confirms each part of a profile in the step that first needs it (the sheet
  conventions with Step 1; the notes with step 2; the level wording with step 3; the grid layers with
  step 4; each family's layers and labels with its step), in bulk like Proposals, with exceptions one by
  one. A convention that would raise the same Question on most sheets is asked once, as a convention of
  kind "convention", never per sheet (Edison's title blocks raised one false Question on each of 57
  sheets).
- **Reused:** the office is proposed from the title block's consultant text and confirmed by the QS
  once per Drawing Set and Discipline. A known office's confirmed profile is applied first; a
  convention the new set breaks becomes a Question, and its answer makes a new profile version. A
  profile never changes silently.
- **Per Discipline:** a set's structural and architectural drawings are usually from different
  offices, revised separately (CONTEXT.md, Discipline); the architectural profile serves step 3's
  levels in M1 and the architectural steps in M2.
- **Where:** `drawings` owns profiles, kept per Developer in M1; `engine/recognise` receives the profile
  as data, and no family's code holds an office's convention.
- **The sheet part's shape is M0's** (review A7): M0's `engine/recognise/types.py` fixes
  `SheetConventions` and `ViewConventions`, shaped as a profile's sheet part, and M0's sheet reader
  already takes them (`sheets.find(artefact, file_discipline, conventions)`) and records them in the
  export as the conventions applied. M1's profiles store, confirm and version values of those types;
  they do not change the reader's signature. M1 adds the other parts (notes, levels, grid, each
  family's layers and labels) beside it.
- **Proof stays honest:** the real-drawing check and the Held-out scoring read every set twice, as an
  unknown office and with its profile (see "Scoring"). A profile is never built from a Held-out Set by a
  build session (ADR 0026). The Development Sets' confirmed profiles carry layer names and label
  patterns, so they live under `.private/`, never in git.
- **Publishing** a profile to the Library, and profiles Vextrus pre-builds for leading Dhaka offices,

### Reading beyond the Sample Project
Evidence: docs/research/edison-check-session-02.md (counts and conventions only). What every reader in
M1 does:
- **The four generic bugs are fixed and stay fixed.** M0's reader applies each insert's
  object-to-world transform (mirrored inserts, extrusion −Z, included), reads MTEXT's angle from its
  direction vector and gives MTEXT with no height inside a block the height its style and insert
  imply (M0 revision item 8). M1 adds the fourth: every geometry repair is guarded, and a repair that
  empties or collapses an outline keeps the original and raises a Question. M1's families carry a
  regression case for each of the four where they meet it (mirrored pile blocks; beam labels at an
  angle).
- **Never a count or a storey total in the reader:** no grid total, typical-floor count, sheet list
  or storey count is written into code or a manifest; N comes from the drawing (ADR 0027), and sources
  that disagree raise a Question. Enforced three ways: the unknown-office read of both Development Sets,
  which differ in size (a literal fitted to one breaks the other); each family's `cloud` tests on
  synthetic sets of two sizes; and review, which treats a literal count as a defect.
- **Schedules drawn as graphics:** where a column schedule is a grid of drawn sections, the column
  family takes each cell's size from the drawn outline and its bars from the drawn dots and callouts,
  and cross-checks both with the cell's text.
- **Notes as free text:** notes may sit in a file of their own, in columns of numbered sections, with
  tables written as text; `spec_note` rebuilds sections and tables from text positions, reads units as
  stated (psi, MPa), and reads laps as bar diameters by member, position and bar-size band.
- **Levels from both disciplines:** step 3 reads the structural and the architectural sections and
  elevations (Edison's structural set carries 3 of its 15 levels); each storey holds its slab level and
  its finished floor level. Where only one is stated, the other is derived from the floor finish the
  notes state, or from one Question, and marked "derived".
- **Layer maps confirmed by geometry, per sheet:** a layer's role is proposed from the profile or a
  dictionary and confirmed by what its geometry is (an outline whose width equals its label's size;
  circles inside caps), never by a thickness score alone (which learnt stair treads and window frames
  as walls on Edison).
- **Labels bound by their own geometry:** anchor, extent and angle against the member's geometry, not a
  fixed distance; "mark (b × d)" expected; a mark never assumed to fix a size (one Edison beam mark
  carries different sizes on different spans).
- **Piles as blocks or circles; marks as types:** a pile is known by its position, and its mark may
  name a type, not a pile.
- **The Edison Checks** (see "The Checks M1 brings") catch the silent wrong answers the Edison check
  found: a floor placed twice, a stale layer, the lift core bound to column marks.

### The family package contract (ADR 0031 §5)
Each package under `engine/families/<family>/` holds four pure functions and a manifest; a registry
is generated from the directory, so a family ticket only adds files.
- **manifest:** key, Discipline Part, Takeoff Step, label, identity rule, milestone, IFC class and
  predefined type, classification references, the facts it carries (each a typed-core field or a
  defined Attribute key, with its figure kind, for the generic Takeoff screen and inspector), the
  Checks it brings, the Measurement Rule codes its measure uses, the Jev nodes it calls, the Drafting
  Profile conventions it proposes, and how its N is counted. Family, Takeoff Step, Check and Attribute
  Definition rows are synced from manifests and Library data, so a family ticket adds no migration.
- **recognise(read artefacts of the views it may use, confirmed facts, project setup, Drafting
  Profile) → candidates + judgement requests + proposed conventions.** Candidates carry values in
  drawing units, verbatim text and per-fact Trace anchors. Judgement requests are closed questions
  over candidates code found (ADR 0011); `takeoff` answers them through Jev or the QS.
- **check(read artefacts, confirmed state) → findings.** Never compared with the pipeline's own
  output (ADR 0027).
- **geometry(confirmed facts, owned solids) → parametric primitives** of three kinds only: an
  extruded polygon with holes, a cylinder, and an extruded polygon along a level offset. The browser
  knows only the primitives, so a family ticket never touches the viewer.
- **measure(confirmed facts, owned solids, Rule Set version) → Measurement Lines** (item code,
  Element, storey, Nos × L × B × H or area, quantity in SI, rule codes, Rebar Basis, diameter, assumed
  flag).

### Takeoff Steps and their state
- **Step Progress:** not started → reading → in review → closed; a closed step is either
  **confirmed** or **closed with Questions open**, and may be reopened.
  - **Confirmed:** every Proposal of its families is confirmed, rejected or excluded with a reason,
    and no Question is open except rebar-only ones, which leave the rebar figure "from the drawing +
    rules" (screens.md, Takeoff ruling 5).
  - **Closed with Questions open** (ADR 0002, Q22): the QS may close a step whose remaining Proposals
    are each held by an open Question, once every failed Element (no candidate) is typed or excluded
    with a reason. Closing writes each held Element through the confirm service at its best candidate
    (the pre-picked one where two sources agree, otherwise the one the reader ranked first) with the
    state "awaiting answer", and replaces the step's allowance by its measured figure. Awaiting-answer
    money shows apart from the measured share in the grid, the whole-building figure and the 3D
    (amber). Answering the Question writes the answered facts and clears the state in one
    Confirmation; any quantity change shows with its reason.
- **Dependencies:** steps 2, 3 and 4 open after step 1; step 5 (piles, pile caps), 6 and later need
  3 and 4 closed; BOQ Items need step 2; step 7 needs 6; step 8 needs 7; pile caps need piles. A step
  may open on a predecessor closed with Questions open. A reopened step, or an answer that moves a
  held Element, re-runs the Checks of every step that depends on it. *(Review Q6: M0 keeps Step 1 per
  Discipline Part. "Step 1" above means the Structural Part's: steps 2–8 open once it is confirmed.
  Step 3 also reads the Architectural Part's sections and elevations, which it can use only once that
  Part's Step 1 is confirmed. A file of a Discipline not yet received opens only its own Part's Step 1;
  it is not a Revision and reopens no step of M1's, so no Structural Check re-runs for it.)*
- **Questions** have a kind (missing, conflict, low confidence, Check, file misread, labour source,
  convention) and a key hashed from the kind, the subject's identity and the evidence's content, so
  an answer is reused only while its evidence is unchanged. **Answering a Question confirms the
  Proposals it held**, in one Confirmation, and the card says so first. A Question is stored as a
  message code and parameters, chosen by the Check or reader that raised it, and worded at the edge
  from the catalogue (ADR 0038); Jev never phrases a Question.
- **Pre-pick:** an answer is pre-picked only when two or more independent sources agree (drawn size,
  label text, schedule cell, per-sheet table), and the card names them.
- **Grouping:** Proposals group by mark within each Storey Band (the band groups the storey pieces of
  columns, shear walls and the core), or by storey for beams and slabs; the bulk Confirmation bar names
  what and why; the exceptions list sorts by effect on quantity, then ৳.
- **Edits and exclusions:** an edited fact becomes a new Proposal with source "QS typed" and a Trace
  of that kind; an exclusion keeps the element in N with its reason. Undo writes an "unconfirm"
  Confirmation and a new Model Version; history is append-only.
- **Coverage:** each view is used by a step, excluded with a reason, assigned to a later step (a view
  carrying only M2 families, such as an architectural wall plan), or unaccounted. Architectural
  sections and elevations are assigned to step 3 as well as to M2's steps. A view whose content a
  recogniser scores as carrying its family, but which the step did not use, raises a Question.
- **Drafting Profiles** replace session 01's "consultant memory": the layer-to-member map and the
  plan-versus-section precedence are parts of the office's profile; Jev is called only for what the
  profile does not know.
- **The confirm service** is the only writer of As designed values in `live_model` (ADR 0035): it
  converts drawing units to SI with exact factors (ADR 0008), checks every `attrs` key and value
  against its Attribute Definition, sets each Element's Construction Stage and casting stage by the
  pinned Rule Set's stage rules, copies Trace anchors, and calls `live_model`'s apply service, which
  opens and closes Element States, records the frame's junction Relations and writes one Model Version.
  Every act writes one domain event.
- **Checks run** when a step's reading finishes and again at every Confirmation, across every step
  whose facts the Confirmation touched.

### As designed Attributes, IFC and classification (ADRs 0035, 0037; Q12, Q14, Q19)

### Measuring (ADRs 0008, 0009, 0031 §1)
- **Measuring is pure:** `measurement.measure(project, facts version, Rule Set version)` reads the As
  designed facts through `live_model` (confirmed Elements, and those awaiting an answer, flagged),
  calls the engine and returns Measurement Lines; it stores nothing. `takeoff` previews it on
  Proposals; `boq` caches it keyed by (the hash of the figure-feeding facts, Rule Set version, Display
  Units) (ADR 0037 §3).
- **The Rule Set version M1 signs** is the Bangladeshi default as Library data for the Bangladesh
  Market: every rule of docs/research/qs-defaults.md §1 as docs/specs/bd-defaults.md sets it, with the
  session-02 refinements (Q23): P3, a grade beam bearing on its cap measured between the faces of the
  columns it connects; E4 extended to grade beams, so FW6 measures no grade-beam soffit; E3, sand
  filling from ground level to the ground-floor slab's underside where no depth is stated, derived,
  with a Question where a section disagrees. MEP1 is left out: ADR 0040 supersedes it, and M3 adds the
  MEP conventions (bd-defaults, Q32) in its own version. Each rule is written in Vextrus's own words,
  citing its clause and never quoting the standard, labelled with its source. M1's families apply the
  frame's rules (G1–G6, F1–F8, FW1–FW6, R1–R4, P1–P2, CA1 and the detailing rules); the rest are
  written and signed now, so that the Hand Takeoffs of all five zones are measured under one version
  and M2's families apply them. The version also holds the BOQ Items and Billing Units, as rows per
  unit system, for the whole starting library (so allowances can name them); the Rebar Ratios; the
  diameter splits; the rounding rules; the Construction Stage and casting-stage rules; the BOQ Section
  rules; and the labour-measure item for casting area (CA1). It is published, immutable and pinned per
  project. Editing it is M2.
- **One junction function** assigns every concrete volume to one element by the Rule Set's joint rule
  (IS 1200 Pt 2 cl. 4.2.2 by default: pile stops at cut-off; column starts at the cap top and stops
  at the slab soffit; the slab runs over beams and columns; a beam runs between column faces below
  the slab). The measurer, the 3D geometry and the viewer's clear spans all call it, and the
  conservation Check proves its result sums to the union. The junctions it finds between confirmed
  Elements are recorded as Element Relations of the structural kind (ADR 0040), so that the relation
  table, its versioning and its Question when one end changes exist before M2 and M3 add hosted in, in
  room, passes through, spans storeys and same thing as. Ownership is always derived from the facts by
  the function, never read back from stored Relations.
- **Measured on what is confirmed:** a column confirmed before the slab above runs to the next level;
  when the slab is confirmed the joint passes to the slab. Totals are conserved; the item split moves
  and shows as a quantity change.
- **Heights and levels** come from step 3's storeys (their slab levels); a sunk panel sits at its
  confirmed local level.
- **Units:** drawings and Proposals stay in drawing units; the Live Model is SI; Measurement Lines are
  computed in SI and converted exactly into each Billing Unit; nothing that feeds a figure is a float.
- **Rounding** as the owner ruled (data-model §6, rulings 4 and 6; ADR 0038): each Measurement Line to
  2 decimals in its Billing Unit and the item their sum; nos and bags whole; tons to 3 decimals; rebar
  lines to 2 decimals of a kg and the rebar item in whole kg; rates to the currency's minor unit (the
  paisa for ৳); amount = ROUND(quantity × rate) to that unit; the Material Schedule rounds up.

### Rebar (ADR 0010)
- **Columns: from the drawing.** The column family reads each band's main bars and ties from the
  schedule, as text or as drawn sections (code parses candidate readings such as "8-20Ø + 4-16Ø" and
  "10Ø @ 6\" c/c", or counts drawn bar dots against their callouts; Jev picks among them only where
  roles are ambiguous), and writes confirmed rebar bars in `live_model`: bar mark, role, diameter,
  count, cutting length, laps, for each storey's piece. Mass is length × standard mass per metre (R1).
- **Detailing rules** supply what the drawing is silent on, each a Measurement Rule of kind "rebar
  detailing" with its parameters, and each assumed length traced to its rule: laps (R3, 40 × d where
  unstated; a lap table in the notes, by member, position and bar-size band, makes laps "from the
  drawing"), tie hooks, anchorage into the pile cap, the top-storey anchorage, where laps fall, how ties
  are counted per spacing zone, and cover where the notes are silent. The defaults are those of open
  question 4, accepted on 26 Sep; the Hand Takeoff uses the same signed rules.
- **Everything else in M1 is by ratio:** piles, pile caps, shear walls, the lift core, beams, slabs
  and slab-edge members, as concrete volume × Rebar Ratio (R2), with the verified diameter split per
  family (bd-defaults, Q13) marked "assumed" in the Material Schedule. Per-Storey-Band ratio overrides
  wait for the editable Rule Set in M2.
- **Rebar Basis per Measurement Line**; a rebar item shows its share from the drawing.
- **Laps** are their own visible Measurement Lines and enter the Material Schedule; a Benchmark on
  net-of-lap kg waits for M2's Benchmark Rates.

### Money (ADRs 0002, 0006, 0028, 0038)
- **Currency:** every money value carries its currency (`{amount, currency}` at the API). A Project's
  currency is its Market's (৳ for Bangladesh), and each Market Price set names its currency. Amounts are
  exact decimals of scale ≥ 3 (amounts `dec(18,4)`), rounded to the currency's minor units (2 for ৳)
  wherever a rule rounds. M1 converts no currency.
- **Formatting:** every figure passes through its kind's formatter, driven by the Market: money and
  quantities grouped in lakh and crore (`1,24,500 kg`), coordinates and dimensions never grouped, and
  drawing notation (marks, grid labels, 14′-6″) isolated left to right. The browser and, from M1, the
  server check their strings against one shared table.
- **Rate Analyses:** the starting library of docs/specs/bd-defaults.md (the 18 items of qs-defaults
  §2.3 plus ready-mix RCC including the pump), plus the piling items open question 2 settled. One
  Rate Analysis per item, shown per 100 cft or 100 sft; editable in place by the Developer, each edit
  an event. Working rate per Billing Unit = Σ materials × (1 + wastage) × Market Price + labour +
  plant, exact, rounded to the currency's minor unit.
- **Market Prices** live in dated sets; edits go into the working set; each price keeps its
  last-changed date. Nothing in M1 freezes a set (Issued Estimates are M2).
- **The starter Market Price set** (Q21; docs/research/pwd-sor-2022-input-prices.md, refuter-checked)
  is the Library's copy of PWD's SoR 2022 (2nd Revised), in force from 22 Jan 2026, Dhaka / Mymensingh
  column, each price citing its SoR page and labelled "PWD prices, replace with yours". The flags travel
  with it: the prices are PWD's 2022–23 prices carried into the 2026 edition; sand is far under the walk
  prices; Grade 500 rebar is printed below Grade 400; brick chips are derived (৳120.20/cft: 850 bricks
  and their breaking per 100 cft); binding wire is PWD's G.I. wire (৳120/kg).
- **The starter labour rates** (Q30; bd-defaults), all Low and labelled, each Developer's own replacing
  them: the casting-area Labour Contract at ৳170 per sft of casting area; the labour-only piling
  contract at ৳450 per rft of boring and casting and ৳345 per pile head; outside a contract, shuttering
  at ৳18/sft and concrete casting at ৳31.50/cft (net of PWD's mark-ups; casting derived); and M2's items'
  labour (brickwork, plaster, tile laying, earthwork, backfill, sand filling), which prices the
  allowances. Rebar binding outside a contract, brick flat soling and painting labour have no figure:
  they show "rate not entered", never ৳0. This replaces the 26 Sep answer to open question 7.
- **A Resource without a Market Price** leaves its items unpriced and shown "rate not entered",
  never ৳0.
- **Labour Contracts** belong to the Developer, with a per-project override of rate or scope; each
  has its own labour-measure BOQ Item (casting area by CA1: each slab's area over beams and columns,
  per floor), its own line at the end of the BOQ Section whose items it covers, and a list of covered
  items whose own labour lines drop out.
- **BOQ Items** come from the Rule Set: the mix decides the item (from step 2 through the Developer's
  strength-to-item choice), the element class groups it, and numbering (section.group.item) is
  derived, never stored. In M1, Sub-structure holds piles, pile caps and columns from the cap top to
  the plinth (a Rule Set section rule); Super-structure holds columns, shear walls and the lift core,
  beams, slabs and slab-edge members. Each Measurement Line names its Element, so M2's Issued Estimates
  can freeze each Element's issued cost (ADR 0028, Q7).
- **Cost Basis per Takeoff Step** (ADR 0002 history, 26 Sep; screens.md, Summary rulings 4 and 7):
  - each step holds an allowance as consumption per sft of Gross Floor Area: a list of (BOQ Item,
    quantity per sft), or ৳ per sft where the step's items are Lump Sums (step 14's site works);
  - the defaults are the verified consumptions of bd-defaults (Q8: tax-and-allowances.md §B.2 on its
    reference building, Low, replaced by the Developer's past projects);
  - its ৳ is that consumption × the Building's Gross Floor Area, priced at the current Market Prices;
  - it shows as one allowance line in the step's BOQ Section, never split, hatched and worded;
  - its source is Vextrus's default (set by the owner) or typed by the QS (recorded);
  - closing the step, confirmed or with Questions open, replaces the allowance whole with the step's
    measured figure (awaiting-answer Elements at their best candidate); reopening a step keeps it
    measured;
  - before then, "measured so far: ৳X of an allowance of ৳Y" shows beside it, and its measured
    items show beneath in a muted group that is not counted;
  - step 5 carries two allowance parts in M1 (open question 1); the plan splits step 5's verified lines
    between them (pile and cap lines, with their rebar at the Rebar Ratios, in the first);
  - **the MEP Parts' allowances** are the MEP template's ৳/sft lines (bd-defaults), until M3 reads each
    Part (ADRs 0007, 0040); fire follows the confirmed storey count: ৳50/sft below 7 storeys and ৳120/sft
    from 7 (net; Q32).
- **The whole-building figure** is the direct cost of the Building: measured + awaiting answer +
  allowance, with the measured share as one bar (awaiting answer beside it, never inside it) and cost
  per sft of Gross Floor Area. The Estimate's layers are M2. It needs a Gross Floor Area: until one
  exists the figure shows "—" with the action to enter it (open question 5).
- **The Material Schedule** is Σ item quantity × Rate Analysis line × (1 + wastage), by Resource,
  storey and Construction Stage, gross, rounded up, in quantities and ৳; Material-and-Labour
  materials are excluded; rebar is split by diameter (read, or "assumed"). Stage rules are Rule Set
  data: piles to piling; pile caps and columns up to the plinth to substructure; each column to the
  casting of the slab above it; beams, slabs and slab-edge members to their own slab casting.
  Allowance materials appear marked (open question 6). Each Resource has an editable procurement lead
  time shown against its first stage.
- **Nothing working is stored:** the Priced BOQ, the Material Schedule and the whole-building figure
  are computed on read from the Measurement Line cache and the current prices.

| A typed answer's unit | regex | the unit, only where regex is unsure |

## Testing Decisions

- **A good test states behaviour at the highest seam that shows it,** never an implementation
  detail. Synthetic fixtures prove mechanics only; they are never offered as proof of reading.
- **Seam 1, the whole chain (highest; the one that matters most):** through `takeoff`'s public
  services and HTTP API, a synthetic project's Proposals are confirmed, and the Priced BOQ, the
  Material Schedule and the whole-building figure are read back through `boq`'s API. It proves the
  typed chain element → Measurement Line → BOQ Item → rate → amount → material end to end: answering
  a Question confirms its elements; excluding keeps N; undo restores the previous Model Version's
  figures; a closed step replaces its allowance; closing with Questions open prices held Elements at
  their best candidate, keeps them out of the measured share, refuses while a failed Element is
  neither typed nor excluded, and answering moves them in; a Market Price edit moves rates, amounts,
  allowances and materials and nothing else; a Labour Contract drops covered labour and the
  labour-source Check fires on a double cover; quantity × rate = amount on every row, rounded to the
  currency's minor unit; every money value carries its currency; switching Display Units re-bills;
  the Material Schedule rounds up.
- **Seam 2, the engine's pure functions:** the measurer and the junction function on synthetic
  frames, with property tests: owned volumes sum exactly to the union for random frames; unit
  conversions are exact; each rule (F1–F8, FW1–FW6, P1–P2, R1–R4 and the detailing rules) has a worked
  example taken from its words, which the owner can read; an Element's clear spans in the viewer's data
  equal the measurer's F3 lengths.
- **Seam 3, the family contract:** each family's recognise and check on small synthetic views for
  mechanics (`cloud`), on synthetic sets of two sizes so that a literal count fails one, and on the
  real sets through the real-drawing check (`local`). The four generic bugs each have a synthetic
  regression case (a mirrored insert with extrusion −Z, an MTEXT at an angle stored as a direction
  vector, MTEXT with no height inside a block, a self-intersecting outline whose repair collapses).
- **Attributes:** the confirm service refuses an undefined `attrs` key and a value of the wrong type or
  dimension; a change to a value that feeds no figure writes a Model Version and leaves the cache key;
  CI checks every IFC name against the pinned IFC 4.3 dictionary and every Uniclass code against the
  pinned edition.
- **Drafting Profiles:** on synthetic sets drawn two ways, a confirmed profile removes the convention
  Questions of the second read and changes no confirmed fact; a convention broken by a new set raises
  one Question and makes a new version; no profile is applied across tenants.
- **The real-drawing check (ADR 0030)** runs on every PR touching `engine` or the reading modules:
  per Development Set, both reads (as an unknown office, and with the set's confirmed profile), n / N
  per Takeoff Step against the owner-confirmed expected N values, the change against the last merged
  run element by element, and the count of changed attributes per family. Aggregates only; a `local`
  reading ticket stops when its n / N stops improving.
- **Reader upgrade:** a `cloud` test on a synthetic set asserts zero Proposals, zero Element States,
  zero Questions, an unchanged facts version and every new anchor resolving; the same assertions run
  `local` on both Development Sets, with and without their profiles.
- **3D:** a CI test compares the browser's geometry with the server's per-element volume and bounds
  (ADR 0022); a measure test asserts snapped distances equal model distances; the budgets are measured
  with `?bench` on the reference setup at the walk.
- **Jev:** tests use recorded answers; live calls run only in tests marked live, never with the cloud
  key on real text; each node's spot check runs `local`.
- **UI:** the design gate (docs/sdlc.md) on every UI PR at 1440×900 and 1280×800 against a seeded
  synthetic project; a browser test drives the Takeoff by keyboard (confirm a band, answer a
  Question, exclude, undo, close with a Question open) and asserts the counts and figures shown. The
  browser test tool is chosen in the plan.
- **Formatters:** screen figures, and from M1 the server's, are checked against the one shared table
  of expected strings (lakh and crore on money and quantities, none on coordinates and dimensions).
- **What `cloud` tests prove:** the mechanics, the arithmetic and the contracts, on synthetic data.
  **What `local` tests prove:** no regression on the Development Sets. **What proves reading:** only
  the Held-out Set, scored blind at the walk.
- **Prior art:** M0's tests (the read job, the row-level security policy test, the real-drawing
  check's regression diff) are the first; M1's follow their layout.

## Further Notes

### Why the M1 / M2 boundary falls where it does
1. **Piles and pile caps are M1; the rest of step 5 is M2** (ADRs 0002, 0007 as amended). Grade beams
   look like beams, but they sit on their own layout with slab-on-grade and earthwork decisions
   that belong together; pulling them in would pull derived earthwork in.
2. **Pile boring and pile-head breaking are measured in M1,** though the milestone line says
   "concrete, formwork, rebar". Confirming piles replaces step 5's piles-and-caps allowance whole; if
   boring were not measured, the whole-building figure would drop by the piling contract's value on
   Confirmation. They are pure functions of pile facts M1 confirms anyway.
3. **The Labour-source Check is M1,** though not in the task's list, because Labour Contracts are M1
   and ADR 0006 makes the Check the condition for them.
4. **Rate Analysis editing is M1;** Rule Set editing is M2. A QS who may not type over a rate
   (screens.md, grid ruling 3) needs a way to correct a coefficient on day one; a rule edit needs
   the re-measure preview that comes with Issued Estimates and Comparisons.
5. **The whole-building figure lives on top of the Priced BOQ in M1,** readable by the MD on desktop;
   the Project Summary and its phone layout are M2, as the milestones table says.
6. **Display Units, the per-floor item layout and procurement lead times are M1:** each is small, and
   each is part of the BOQ or Material Schedule M1 delivers (ADRs 0008, 0002; grid ruling 7).
7. **The stair and lift tower's frame is M1** (its columns, core, beams and slabs are steps 6–8); the
   stair itself, the lift pit, parapets and roof treatment are M2.
8. **All of step 8 is M1,** slab-edge members included, because a step's allowance is replaced whole
   on its Confirmation: a partial step 8 would drop class (d) money.
9. **The viewer's checking tools are M1; its drawings, placed notes and presentation are M2** (Q25): a
   QS checks the frame as it is confirmed, while placed dimensions and notes must survive Revisions and
   presentation needs the owner's bench.
10. **Drafting Profiles are learnt, confirmed and reused inside a Developer's data in M1;** publishing
    waits for a client's written permission (ADR 0039, milestone M5).
11. **Attribute Definitions are written for M1's families only;** each later family brings its own,
    with no schema change (ADR 0037).
12. **The whole default Rule Set is signed in M1,** so that one version serves every Hand Takeoff zone
    and M2's families apply rules that already exist.

## Open questions for the owner (one at a time, recommendation first)

1. **Step 5's allowance in M1.** Recommendation: hold it in two parts, "piles and pile caps"
   (replaced when M1's part of step 5 is confirmed) and "rest of foundations and substructure"
   (allowance until M2), both in Sub-structure. Otherwise confirming piles either wipes out the money
   for grade beams and earthwork or keeps an allowance over measured piles. This refines "replaced
   whole when the step is confirmed" for the one step M1 splits.
2. **How piling is priced.** Recommendation: pile concrete by its own Rate Analysis (materials, with
   the over-cast as a visible line), boring measured in rft and pile-head breaking per pile, both
   priced by a default Labour Contract "piling per rft" whose rate the Developer enters. Dhaka
   Developers usually let piling to a piling contractor; you know whether it is labour-only or
   material-and-labour.
3. **Counting beams and slab panels for "counts exact".** Recommendation: a beam is one span between
   supports (matching rule F3 and identity by axis segment); a slab panel is one region bounded by
   beams or edges with one thickness and level. Written into the Rule Set, so the Hand Takeoff counts
   the same way.
4. **Column rebar detailing defaults** (with R3's 40 × d laps), from practice, unverified, each a
   parameter: tie hooks 135° with 6 × d extension, at least 75 mm (the ACI 318 seismic hook, clause
   to verify); one lap per bar per storey, above the slab; column bars anchored into the pile cap to
   its bottom mat with a 12 × d bend; top-storey bars to the top of the roof slab less cover with a
   12 × d bend; ties counted per stated spacing zone as length ÷ spacing + 1, continuing through the
   beam depth; no bend deductions; cover 40 mm where the notes are silent. Recommendation: accept, or
   correct any line, before the Hand Takeoff of columns.
5. **The Gross Floor Area in M1.** Recommendation: use the QS-entered figure from the area statement
   until walls are confirmed in M2, with the area measured from confirmed slabs shown beside it and
   flagged if the two differ by more than 5 %. The Gross Floor Area is defined to the outside of the
   walls, which M1 does not measure.
6. **Allowance materials in the Material Schedule.** Recommendation: yes, marked "allowance" and
   hatched, in the stage the step feeds and in one "not yet by floor" column until the storeys'
   areas are known. Cement and rebar totals are then whole-building from the first Confirmation, like
   the ৳.
7. **The starter Market Price set.** Recommendation: ship PWD SoR 2022 (2nd Revised) material input
   prices as the starter set, cited and dated, labelled "PWD prices, replace with yours"; ship no
   labour rates, so labour shows "rate not entered" until the Developer enters its Labour Contracts.
   The alternative is an empty set, with no figure until the Vextrus Engineer enters prices.
8. **Rebar from the drawing for shear walls and the lift core** is in no milestone (ADR 0010 names
   columns, beams and slabs). Recommendation: add it to M3 after slabs, so ratio rebar is not the
   permanent answer for the core, as your condition requires.
9. **A pass mark for the timed Takeoff.** Recommendation: none in M1; record time, Confirmations and
   Questions per step, and set it beside the outside QS's own estimate of a manual frame takeoff for
   the same set. Setting a mark before the first measurement would be a guess.
10. **How a stated concrete strength becomes a BOQ Item.** Recommendation: the Developer keeps a table
    of strength to BOQ Item (a site Mix such as 1:1.5:3, or ready-mix of a grade), pre-picked in step
    2 per element class and remembered for its next project, because consultants' notes state f′c
    while the items and Rate Analyses are by Mix.
