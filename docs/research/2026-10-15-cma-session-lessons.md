# One Managed Agents session: what it did, what it cost, and what made it work

15 Oct 2026. One Claude Managed Agents session on the Claude Platform (`docs/runbooks/cma-prototype-session/`)
dissected the Edison set and built a 3D prototype read from it. This file records what happened, with the
figures from the session's own status and events, so the next local workflow can copy what worked and
avoid what didn't. It holds no drawing content.

## What it delivered
- **Outcome 1, the dissection: passed on grading round 2.** Five Disciplines got a report each, with the
  office's conventions counted. The Element inventory has 3,579 rows (7,585 storey pieces): 3,305 `read`,
  258 `inferred`, 16 `placeholder`, each with a Trace (file, layer, handles, sheet). The grader opened 30
  random handles in the DXFs, and all 30 sat on the layers named. It also produced one storey datum
  (14 storeys), one 8-axis grid with each Discipline's registration, 13 cross-Discipline mismatches
  listed, and 3,864 Element Relations by kind. The engine's own readers were run on all 7 files, with
  their gaps listed per Takeoff Step. A QS critic review (15 findings), an Adversary check of 24 Traces,
  8 owner questions, and an ADR proposal for the name "BIM Live Model" complete it.
- **Outcome 2, the prototype: built, but the last grading round was cut off by the budget.** It is a
  static web app on three.js 0.184. Its Live Model is generated by code from the inventory, so a
  regenerated model hashes the same. 1,225 of 1,225 figures open a Trace, and 1,207 of those open an
  Element or a measurement line. It walks the Takeoff, the Priced BOQ, the Material Schedule and the
  MD's figure, with Life Phase and 4D/5D previews. Its public generator has a synthetic fixture and
  passing tests.
- **The owner's verdict on opening it:** "lots of issues, lack of quality": Elements misplaced and
  detached from their hosts, and parts poorly done. The grader passed the rubric's letter; the owner's eye
  failed the product. A rubric that checks files and counts does not check that a beam sits on its
  columns.

## What it cost
| Step | List cost | Wall clock |
|---|---|---|
| Two smoke checks | $0.59 | minutes |
| Outcome 1 | $105.22 | about 2 h (6,944 s of agent activity) |
| Outcome 2 | the rest of the $200 credit | about 3 h |

The estimate for outcome 1 was $40-70. The overrun came from three things. Environment repair: no
cost per step was reported, so its share is unmeasured; it ran at the Lead's `xhigh` rates and took
much of the first 45 minutes, which cost $58: LibreDWG built from source, uv and Python 3.14 upgraded, bwrap installed. Five
dissectors and two researchers ran in parallel at about $70 an hour. And there was a second grading
round, partly because the Lead never saw the rubric.

## What made it work (copy these)
1. **The work was framed as an outcome with a rubric, and graded by someone who didn't do it.** The
   grader ran in its own context, read the files itself, sampled the evidence (30 handles, `diff -rq` of
   the archive) and sent back specific gaps. This did more for quality than any instruction to "be
   thorough".
2. **One coordinator with fresh-context specialists.** The Lead kept the plan and the synthesis. Each
   dissector got one Discipline, a common brief file (`BRIEF-common.md`: one Element schema, one origin,
   one unit) and its own folder. Their reports came back small; the Lead's context stayed clean.
3. **Independent critics before the grade.** The QS critic and the Adversary found real defects: piles
   priced by volume instead of length, 85 Trace fixes. The grader then found fewer.
4. **A checkpoint file as memory.** `CHECKPOINT.md` held the state, decisions, deferred items and paths,
   and was rewritten at each milestone. When the context was compacted, nothing was lost.
5. **Evidence states on every Element** (`read`, `inferred`, `placeholder`, each with its rule). They made
   the model honest and checkable, and the grader could count them.
6. **No ceremony.** No PRs, no status posting, no merge gates during the work: the agents spent their
   tokens on the product. Review happened as part of the work, not as a separate pipeline after it.
7. **The agent looked at its own output.** Screenshots in headless Chromium were taken, read and acted
   on. They were slow (60-80 s each in software WebGL), so they were budgeted.

## What went wrong (avoid these)
1. **The rubric reached only the grader.** The platform gives an outcome's rubric to the grader alone,
   so the Lead worked from a one-paragraph description and wrote to its own paths. Any builder must see
   the exact acceptance criteria it will be judged on.
2. **The rubric judged files, not the building.** It never asked whether Elements sit where the drawing
   puts them, touch their hosts, or close the frame. That is why misplaced and detached Elements passed.
   The next rubric needs geometric invariants checked by code (every beam end on a column or wall, every
   column under the slab above it, every hosted Element inside its host, no floating Element) and a side
   by side of each storey's model plan against the drawing's plan.
3. **The grader judged a stale archive.** Round 3's feedback was about `outputs.tar.gz` as it stood an
   hour earlier. A grader must read the live files.
4. **Environment repair inside the expensive loop.** Toolchain work (building LibreDWG, upgrading uv and
   Python) ran at Opus `xhigh` prices. A prepared environment would have avoided it; locally, the
   toolchain already exists under `/opt/vextrus`.
5. **The budget was set by guess.** Outcome 1 cost about 2.5 times its estimate. Phase caps, the session
   pausing at its cap, and the owner raising it phase by phase kept the overrun visible, and that worked.

## What carries over to local Claude Code
- Claude Code has every piece the session used: subagents with their own model, effort and tools;
  parallel background agents; dynamic workflows (the `Workflow` tool, `.claude/workflows/`) for
  fan-out, pipelines and adversarial verification; Chromium through the chrome-devtools MCP; and the
  real GPU on the owner's machine, where screenshots take seconds, not a minute.
- What the platform did for us, and the local setup must do itself:
  - The independent grader: a read-only subagent given the rubric and the live files, never the
    builder's notes.
  - The rubric-and-revise loop.
  - The dollar cap. A Max subscription has usage limits instead; the session's equivalent is a stated
    budget of agents and rounds.
