# The business: the Developer first, priced per project, Bangladesh first

**The customer.** Vextrus's first paying customer is the Bangladeshi real-estate Developer: its MD
buys, and its QS uses the product daily. The Developer holds the full Drawing Set, measures and prices
every project, and runs the whole chain (estimate → procurement → site → cost control) that the one
project dataset serves. The founding team's university network already reaches Developers waiting to
see the product. Consultants and contractors are later customers of the same dataset.

**The price.** A Developer pays per project, ৳4 per sft of Gross Floor Area (ADR 0016), with a
minimum of ৳60,000 per building; a typical 25,000 sft G+9 building comes to about ৳1 lakh, today's
price for a bought estimate. Revised drawings of the same building are included for 6 months. The
first three Developers (founding clients) pay half, in exchange for: permission to use their Drawing
Sets locally as Independent Sets (ADR 0005); a feedback session after each Takeoff; a reference if
satisfied. Their first Takeoff is "done with you": a Vextrus Engineer sits with their QS. A firm
subscription comes later, once something lives across projects (Rate Analyses, Market Prices, the
Rule Set, project memory, cost control).

**The QS's time is measured.** Vextrus records time per Takeoff Step and Confirmations and Questions
per step, as telemetry for the owner, never shown to the client. The M1 and M2 walks each include a
timed Takeoff by a QS who did not build the product. The owner may revisit the price with those
timings after M2.

**The market.** Bangladesh only until 10 Developers pay and at least 5 of them buy a second project.
Growth runs through founding clients' references, REHAB fairs (where the Sample Project is shown) and
design consultants. The next market is then chosen by research: the Gulf leads, India later, the UK
and Australia last (BIM mandates, their own standards). From day one everything that differs by
country is data: Rule Sets, Rate Analyses, currency, Display Units, language.

**Why.** The Developer buys a result, not a tool; per-project pricing scales with the building, and
Dhaka Developers dislike yearly subscriptions. Most estimate in-house, where the cost is salaries and
weeks of slow work; cutting that time is the lever in the bargain.

**Rejected.** Tender contractors (they usually receive the BOQ and only price it), design consultants
(thin fees) and freelance QS firms (low willingness to pay) as first customers; per seat per year
(disliked, and it prices the tool, not the result).
